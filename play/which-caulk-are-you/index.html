Which caulk are you?

Nine questions. Eight formulations. One deeply personal result about how flexible, paintable or clingy you really are.

You are

Acrylic Latex

The dependable friend who helps you paint.

You're everywhere, and people take you for granted. That's fine. You know the truth: half the interiors in America are held together by your quiet competence.

You clean up with water, you're ready for paint in no time, and you make everyone's trim look sharp. You're not a fan of the rain, and you'll admit you shrink a little under pressure. Nobody's perfect.

Where you shine in real life

  • Baseboards, crown molding and interior window trim
  • Gaps between drywall and door or window casings
  • Any interior joint you plan to paint
  • Dry rooms: bedrooms, living rooms, hallways

Read up on acrylic latex

You are

Siliconized Acrylic

The diplomat.

You're the middle ground, and proud of it. A little silicone in your blood makes you more flexible and more water-tolerant than your plain-latex cousin, but you'll still take paint and clean up with water.

You're who people call when they want “good enough for the kitchen” without committing to forever. Just don't volunteer for the steam shower.

Where you shine in real life

  • Kitchen and bath trim that sees the occasional splash
  • Interior window frames and sills
  • Sink and counter edges you want to paint
  • General-purpose interior sealing

Read up on siliconized acrylic

You are

100% Silicone

The one with the cape.

Water doesn't scare you. Steam doesn't scare you. You're flexible, you bond like a champion to glass, tile and metal, and you'll stay exactly who you are for years.

Nobody is painting over you, and honestly, they shouldn't try. You can be a little hard to work with when rushed, but in a crisis, you're the first one people call.

Where you shine in real life

  • Tubs, showers and shower doors
  • Sinks, faucets and countertops
  • Glass, metal and glazed tile
  • Wet zones and exterior joints that won't be painted

Read up on 100% silicone

You are

Polyurethane

The tough one.

You're built for the hard jobs: concrete, masonry, siding, driveways. You grip like you mean it, you shrug off sun and weather, and you'll even take paint.

You need your space, a little extra time, and good airflow, and you don't apologize for it. When you commit, you commit for years.

Where you shine in real life

  • Concrete expansion joints and driveway cracks
  • Masonry, brick and stone
  • Exterior wood and siding joints
  • Garage floors (the self-leveling kind)

Read up on polyurethane

You are

Butyl Rubber

The loyal clinger.

Glamour isn't your thing. Sticking around is. You bond to metal, masonry and wood, you laugh at bad weather, and you've been quietly holding roofs and RVs together for decades.

You're a bit messy, you take forever to settle down, and your smell enters the room before you do. The people who need you really, really need you.

Where you shine in real life

  • Gutter seams and end caps
  • Flashing and roof penetrations
  • RVs, trailers and campers
  • Metal-to-metal joints outdoors

Read up on butyl rubber

You are

Elastomeric Latex

The bendy one.

You go with the flow. Summer, winter, expansion, contraction: you stretch, you recover, and you stay bonded while everything around you shifts.

You're easy to get along with, you take paint, and you forgive a lot. Just don't ask you to sit underwater all day.

Where you shine in real life

  • Exterior siding and trim
  • Stucco and masonry cracks that move
  • High-movement exterior joints
  • Anywhere outdoors you want flexible and paintable

Read up on elastomeric latex

You are

Acoustic Sealant

The quiet one.

You never fully harden, and that's your superpower. You stay soft, you fill the gaps other people don't notice, and the result is peace and quiet.

You're not a fan of water and you prefer to work behind the scenes, inside the walls, where nobody sees you. The people who live with you just notice they're sleeping better.

Where you shine in real life

  • Gaps around electrical boxes in sound-rated walls
  • Top and bottom plates in soundproofed walls
  • Home theaters, studios and nurseries
  • Drywall perimeter seams in quiet rooms

Read up on acoustic sealant

You are

MS Polymer

The overachiever.

Paintable like latex, waterproof like silicone, and you stick to basically everything: wood, metal, concrete, plastic, glass. You're who the pros call when a job can't fail.

You skip the isocyanates polyurethane carries around, too. Yes, you cost more. Excellence usually does.

Where you shine in real life

  • Joints between different materials
  • High-end exterior projects
  • Windows, doors and siding you'll paint
  • One tube for (nearly) everything outside

Read up on MS polymer

The fine print

This quiz is for fun. The “where you shine” lists are real, though, so if your result matches the job in front of you, you're in luck. When the job matters, the Caulk Finder and the label on the tube get the final say.
