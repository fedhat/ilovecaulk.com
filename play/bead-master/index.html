Mouse or touch: hold to squeeze and steer the nozzle along the seam. Keyboard: click the job site or tab to it, turn on steady-hand mode, hold Space to squeeze and use the arrow keys to set your hand speed. R pops the pressure release, Enter finishes a step, Esc pauses.

How to play

  1. Pick a job

    Start at the baseboard and work your way up to the gutter. Each job gets one tube, so wasted caulk can leave you short.

  2. Squeeze and steer

    Hold the mouse button (or your finger, or Space) to squeeze. The gun takes a beat to build pressure, so start moving as soon as caulk appears. Move too slowly and you'll lay a blobby bead. Move too fast and it thins out and skips.

  3. Stop the ooze

    Letting go of the trigger doesn't stop the flow right away. Ease off a moment before the end of the seam and keep moving, or hit R to pop the pressure release. On touch screens, lifting your finger pops the release for you.

  4. Tool the bead

    After you press Done caulking, drag along the bead (or hold Space) to smooth it. Tooling evens out lumps, pushes caulk into the joint and wipes up strays, and it earns a bonus.

  5. Read the scorecard

    You're graded on coverage, consistency, neatness and time, with a bonus for tooling. The crew adds one lesson to take to a real joint.

Troy's tip

The gutter job is windy. Watch the nozzle tip, not the bead behind it, and correct early.

What the game teaches you

Speed sets the bead

With steady pressure, your hand speed decides the bead size. A real bead should just bridge the gap with a slight crown. Pick one pace and hold it.

Corners thin out

Hands speed up through turns without meaning to. Slow down at corners and keep squeezing. That's where most real leaks start.

Guns keep oozing

A classic ratchet gun keeps pushing after you let go. Release a beat early, or use the thumb release. A dripless gun helps a lot.

Tooling makes the seal

Running a wet finger or tooling spatula along the bead presses caulk into both sides of the joint and levels the lumps. It isn't just for looks.

Waste costs you

Caulk that misses the joint does nothing but make cleanup harder. Keep the tip riding in the seam, held at about 45 degrees.

The right caulk still matters

A perfect bead of the wrong formulation still fails. Silicone goes in the tub, paintable acrylic on the baseboard, and something tough goes on the gutter.
