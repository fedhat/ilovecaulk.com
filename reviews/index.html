How we review

These are editorial summaries, not lab tests. Each one pulls together the manufacturer's own specs and the experience widely reported by pros and DIYers, then judges the tube against the job it was made for. A 4.5 for a roof sealant means it's excellent on a roof, not that you should put it around your tub. No brand pays us. The "Check price on Amazon" buttons are affiliate links: if you buy through one, we may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you, and it never changes a score. Product names and formulas change, so read the tube before you buy.

Kitchen, bath & trim

The tubes that live under the sink and in the painter's bucket.

Siliconized acrylic latex

DAP Alex Plus

Acrylic Latex Caulk Plus Silicone

4.5 out of 5

The painter's default for a reason: cheap, easy, and invisible under a coat of paint.

Best for
Baseboards, crown molding, door and window casings, drywall-to-trim gaps, anything you'll paint.
Skip it if
The joint sits in water (tubs, showers, sink rims) or moves a lot with the seasons.
Paintable?
Yes, quickly
Wet areas?
Splashes only
Where
Interior; light-duty exterior
Cleanup
Water, while it's wet
Timing
DAP advertises paint-ready times of about half an hour. Deep beads take longer; check the tube.

What people love

  • Easy to gun and tool, even for first-timers
  • Soap-and-water cleanup
  • Takes paint without drama
  • Inexpensive and on every shelf

Common gripes

  • Shrinks in deep gaps, so big joints can need a second pass
  • Can crack in joints that move a lot
  • Not the right tube for wet zones, even though it handles humidity

100% silicone

GE Silicone 2 Kitchen & Bath

Sold lately as GE Advanced Silicone 2

4.5 out of 5

The tub-and-shower default. Truly waterproof, stays flexible, and resists mildew.

Best for
Tub-to-tile and shower pan joints, sink rims, backsplash-to-counter seams, anywhere water stands.
Skip it if
You plan to paint it, or the old bead left silicone residue you won't clean off.
Paintable?
No
Wet areas?
Yes
Where
Interior (GE sells window-and-door versions for outside)
Cleanup
Dry rag or mineral spirits before it cures; cured silicone has to be cut and scraped off
Timing
GE advertises fast water-ready times on its kitchen and bath formulas. A full day before a long hot shower is the safe bet.

What people love

  • Genuinely waterproof
  • Stays flexible for years
  • Mildew-resistant formula
  • White and clear versions are easy to find

Common gripes

  • Harder to tool neatly than latex; practice on cardboard
  • Can't be painted
  • Won't bond to old silicone residue, so prep is everything

Solvent-based elastomer

Sashco Lexel

Clear elastomeric sealant

4.0 out of 5

The clear caulk pros keep in the truck. It sticks to things other caulks slide off.

Best for
Clear seals on glass, metal, many plastics, wood, and fixtures, inside or out.
Skip it if
You can't ventilate well, or the gap is wide and deep (it shrinks a little as it dries).
Paintable?
Sashco lists it as paintable
Wet areas?
Yes, once cured
Where
Interior and exterior
Cleanup
Mineral spirits while wet
Timing
Skins fast but can look cloudy for days before it turns fully clear. Check the tube for water exposure.

What people love

  • About as clear as caulk gets
  • Bonds to a long list of surfaces, including many plastics
  • Very flexible

Common gripes

  • Strong solvent smell while it cures
  • Stringy and tricky to tool
  • Cloudy until fully cured, which alarms people

Use it in: Windows & doors, Exterior & outdoors, Bathroom

MS polymer / hybrid

Soudal Fix ALL

Hybrid sealant and adhesive

4.5 out of 5

The modern all-rounder: one tube for wood, metal, masonry, and most plastics.

Best for
Mixed-material seams inside or out, damp surfaces, jobs where you'd rather not choose between paintable and waterproof.
Skip it if
The plastic is polyethylene, polypropylene, or PTFE (hardly anything sticks to those), or the joint stays underwater permanently.
Paintable?
Yes; test oil-based paints
Wet areas?
Yes
Where
Interior and exterior
Cleanup
Before it cures, with a solvent wipe or the maker's cleaning wipes
Timing
Skins in minutes and cures from the outside in, a few millimeters a day. Deep joints take longer.

What people love

  • Sticks to almost everything
  • No isocyanates and little odor
  • Paintable and waterproof
  • Barely shrinks

Common gripes

  • Pricier than traditional caulk
  • Availability varies by region and store
  • Firm grip means mistakes are hard to undo

Use it in: Exterior & outdoors, Garage, Windows & doors

Sikaflex-1a

Polyurethane joint sealant

4.5 out of 5

Concrete's best friend on vertical joints. Tough, pro-grade, and built to move.

Best for
Vertical concrete and masonry joints, foundation-to-siding gaps, precast, and window perimeters in masonry.
Skip it if
The joint is horizontal and needs to flow flat (use the self-leveling version), or it's glass or a tub.
Paintable?
Yes, after cure
Wet areas?
Yes
Where
Exterior and interior
Cleanup
Mineral spirits before it cures; cured sealant must be cut out
Timing
Tack-free within hours, but it cures from the outside in, so deep joints take days.

What people love

  • Very strong grip on concrete and masonry
  • Tough, durable, and flexible
  • Rated to ASTM C920 for joint movement

Common gripes

  • Sticky mess if you're sloppy
  • Stiff to gun when it's cold; a high-thrust gun helps
  • Needs backer rod and clean, dry joints to perform

Skip it if
The joint is vertical or sloped (it will run right out).
Paintable?
Usually left unpainted
Wet areas?
Yes, once cured
Where
Exterior and garage floors
Cleanup
Mineral spirits before it cures

Fast-setting patching cement

4.0 out of 5

An emergency responder, not a joint sealant. It stops the trickle today.

Best for
Plugging active leaks in concrete and block walls, floor-wall joints, and around pipe penetrations.
Skip it if
The crack moves, or the leak comes from a drainage problem you haven't fixed.
Paintable?

Wet areas?

Where
Basements and masonry
Cleanup

What people love

  • Cheap and easy to find
  • Works where no caulk would stick

Common gripes

  • Rigid, so moving cracks reopen
  • Sets so fast you work in small batches
  • Treats the symptom, not the drainage
