Caulk by type

The chemistry on the label decides where a caulk will shine and where it'll fail. Here's every formulation worth knowing, one spec sheet each. For the long version, read Federico's guide to caulk formulations.

  • Acrylic latex
  • Siliconized acrylic latex
  • 100% silicone
  • Polyurethane
  • MS polymer / hybrid
  • Elastomeric latex
  • Butyl rubber
  • Acoustic sealant
  • Hydraulic cement
  • Specialty

Show me caulk that's:

Acrylic latexTrim & drywall

The reliable workhorse

Acrylic latex

  • Paintable
  • Water cleanup

Use it for

Interior trim, baseboards, crown molding, drywall gaps, anything you'll paint.

Skip it for

Showers, tubs, sinks, and exterior joints that move a lot.

Paintable
Yes, often within an hour or so
Water resistance
Moderate once cured
Flexibility
Low to medium; shrinks a little
Cleanup
Soap and water while wet
Feels like
Easy, forgiving, and cheap

Siliconized acrylic latexDamp trim, windows

The upgrade

Siliconized acrylic latex

  • Paintable
  • Water cleanup

Use it for

Kitchen and bath trim, interior window frames, and humid rooms where you still want to paint.

Skip it for

Joints that sit in water, like the tub-to-tile seam.

Paintable
Yes
Water resistance
Good
Flexibility
Medium
Cleanup
Soap and water while wet
Feels like
Acrylic with a raincoat

Our picks: DAP Alex Plus Read the deep dive

100% siliconeTubs, sinks, glass

The gold standard for wet work

100% silicone

  • OK in wet areas
  • Outdoors
  • Handles movement

Use it for

Tubs, showers, sinks, countertops, glass, metal, and other slick, non-porous surfaces.

Skip it for

Anything you'll paint, and joints with old silicone residue you can't fully remove.

Paintable
No (a few specialty formulas claim it)
Water resistance
Excellent
Flexibility
Very high
Cleanup
Wipe off wet with a dry rag, then mineral spirits; cured silicone must be cut off
Feels like
Slippery to tool and totally waterproof

Acetoxy-cure silicones smell like vinegar while curing and can mark some metals and stone; neutral-cure formulas are gentler.

Our picks: GE Silicone 2 Kitchen & Bath, Gorilla 100% Silicone Read the deep dive

PolyurethaneConcrete, masonry

The exterior specialist

Polyurethane

  • Paintable
  • OK in wet areas
  • Outdoors
  • Handles movement

Use it for

Concrete joints, driveways, masonry, foundations, and exterior joints between different materials.

Skip it for

Quick indoor jobs where you want water cleanup, and poorly ventilated rooms.

Paintable
Yes
Water resistance
Excellent
Flexibility
High
Cleanup
Solvent while wet; wear gloves
Feels like
Sticky, tough, and in it for the long haul

Some polyurethanes contain isocyanates. Ventilate, wear gloves, and read the safety sheet.

  • Paintable
  • OK in wet areas
  • Outdoors

Excellent
Flexibility

The roofing and flashing legend

Butyl rubber

  • OK in wet areas
  • Outdoors

Use it for

Gutter seams, metal flashing, roofing details, and other metal-to-metal joints outdoors.

Skip it for

Indoor finish work. It's stringy, slow to cure, and smelly.

Water resistance
Excellent
Flexibility
Medium
Cleanup

Acoustic sealantQuiet walls

The quiet one

Acoustic sealant

  • Handles movement

Use it for

Soundproofing: drywall perimeters, electrical boxes, and gaps in shared walls, floors, and home theaters.

Skip it for

Wet areas and exposed finish surfaces. Hide it behind trim or drywall.

Paintable
Usually not a finish surface; check the label
Water resistance
Moderate
Flexibility
Permanently soft
Cleanup
Varies by product; many clean up with water while wet
Feels like
It never quite dries, on purpose

Our picks: OSI SC-175 Acoustical Read the deep dive

Hydraulic cementActive leaks

The emergency responder

Hydraulic cement

  • Paintable
  • OK in wet areas

Use it for

Plugging active leaks and holes in concrete and masonry. It sets in minutes, even against running water.

Skip it for

Joints that move. It's rigid and will crack.

Paintable
Yes, once cured
Water resistance
Excellent
Flexibility
None
Cleanup
Water, before it sets (which is fast)
Feels like
Mixing a very small, very urgent batch of concrete

Not technically a caulk. It earns its spot on the shelf anyway.

Specialty tubes

Variations built for one job, and they do it better than anything else on the shelf.

  • Self-leveling polyurethane

    Pours into flat concrete joints and levels itself: driveways, garage floors, patios.

    Try: Sikaflex Self-Leveling

  • Sanded tile caulk

    Has sand in it so it matches sanded grout at tile corners and changes of plane.

    Try: Polyblend Sanded Tile Caulk

  • Roof sealant

    Built for asphalt, flashing, and UV; some can go on damp surfaces.

    Try: Sashco Through the Roof!

  • Tripolymer

    A solvent-based sealant that grips dirty, damp, or oily metal, vinyl, and masonry. Gutters love it.

    Try: Geocel 2300 Tripolymer

  • Clear elastomer

    Solvent-based, crystal clear, and very stretchy. Clearer than clear silicone.

    Try: Sashco Lexel

  • High-performance exterior sealant

    Premium window, door, and siding sealants built for extreme weather and every substrate.

    Try: OSI Quad Max

  • Fire-rated sealant

    Seals penetrations through fire-rated walls and floors; some expand in a fire (intumescent). Code rules apply.

    Try: 3M Fire Barrier CP 25WB+
