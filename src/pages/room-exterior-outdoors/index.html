Taking it outside

Exterior caulk has to survive what the interior never will: direct UV, rain, wind, and temperature swings of 100°F or more between summer and winter. For siding and trim, go elastomeric or tripolymer (paintable). For masonry and stucco, choose a polyurethane or silicone. For joints that will move significantly, only a high-movement-rated sealant will last.

Re-caulk exterior trim before painting

  1. Wash and let it dry

    Dirt and chalky paint ruin adhesion. Give washed siding a dry day or two.

  2. Scrape and remove failed caulk

    Anything cracked or loose comes out. Scrape loose paint too.

  3. Prime bare wood

    Primer gives the caulk and the paint something solid to grab.

  4. Backer rod, then caulk

    Rod for joints over 1/4 inch; then a paintable exterior sealant, tooled into the joint.

  5. Paint on schedule

    Paint when the tube allows. Painted sealant lasts longer in the sun.

Rosie's tip

Never caulk the bottom edge of lap siding or trim drip edges. Water that gets behind needs a way out.

Mistakes we see

  • Using caulk as flashing. Caulk is backup; metal and membranes do the real work.
  • Caulking the laps of lap siding, which traps water and rots the wall.
  • Caulking in hot direct sun or right before rain.
  • Skipping primer on bare wood.

Exterior & outdoors questions

Can I caulk before rain?

Only with a product rated for it. Many sealants advertise a rain-ready time; check the tube and the forecast.

What caulk goes on brick?

Polyurethane or a hybrid rated for masonry, with backer rod in wide joints.

Can I paint silicone outside?

Standard silicone won't hold paint. If it needs paint, use a hybrid, polyurethane, or paintable elastomeric instead.
