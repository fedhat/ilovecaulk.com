Even the bedroom needs a good caulking

Bedrooms aren't exactly a moisture battleground, but gaps around windows, baseboards, and crown molding can let in drafts, noise, and pests. A paintable acrylic latex caulk is the workhorse here: easy to apply, dries fast, and disappears under paint. For noise-sensitive households, acoustic caulk is the unsung hero.

Where to caulk in the bedroom

Every joint in the room, what goes in it, and why. The color bar matches the formulation.

Baseboard to wall

Acrylic latex

Closes the dark gap above the baseboard so paint lines look crisp. Easy, fast, and water cleanup.

Don't caulk baseboard to a hardwood floor. The floor needs room to move.

Our pick: DAP Alex Plus

Crown molding

Acrylic latexElastomeric latex

Standard acrylic for most gaps. If a gap opens every winter as the ceiling rises and falls, switch to a high-stretch elastomeric.

Our pick: DAP Alex Plus, Sashco Big Stretch

Window and door casing

Acrylic latex

Seals the casing to the wall and stops small drafts sneaking around the trim.

Our pick: DAP Alex Plus

Outlets and switches on outside walls

Acrylic latex

Seal the gap between the box and the drywall and add foam gaskets under the cover plates. Cold drafts through outlets are real.

Keep caulk out of the electrical box itself. Turn the breaker off first.

Our pick: DAP Alex Plus

Shared walls and noisy gaps

Acoustic sealant

Sound travels through air gaps. Acoustic sealant stays soft and seals the perimeter of drywall and the backs of electrical boxes.

Our pick: OSI SC-175 Acoustical

Nail holes and dings

Not a caulk job

Use wood filler or spackle, not caulk. Caulk shrinks into the hole and shows through the paint.

Caulk baseboards like a painter

  1. Vacuum and wipe

    Dust is the enemy of adhesion. Vacuum the top of the baseboard and wipe it with a barely damp cloth.

  2. Cut a tiny nozzle

    About 1/8 inch. Trim gaps are small, and a small opening gives you control.

  3. Pull the gun steadily

    Work in arm-length runs at an even pace, with the nozzle riding the joint.

  4. Wipe with a damp finger or sponge

    One smooth pass. Keep a bucket of water and a rag nearby; latex cleans up with water while wet.

  5. Paint when the tube says

    Many acrylics are paintable within an hour or so. Check the label, then paint right over it.

Karen's tip

Prime, caulk, then paint. Caulk sticks better to primed wood, and the topcoat hides the line where caulk meets trim.

Mistakes we see

  • Using silicone on trim. It won't take paint, and removing it is miserable.
  • Filling a big gap in one pass. Latex shrinks; fill deep gaps in two passes or use backer rod.
  • Caulking nail holes. That's a filler's job.
  • Chasing the winter crown-molding crack with plain acrylic every year.

How long it lasts

When you repaint, or when a line of shadow appears.

Many years indoors, since trim doesn't see water.

Bedroom questions

Why does the crack at my crown molding come back every winter?

In many houses the ceiling rises and falls with the seasons (often called truss uplift). Plain caulk can't stretch that far. Use a high-stretch elastomeric caulk, or ask a carpenter about fastening the crown only to the ceiling so it moves with it.

Caulk before or after painting?

After priming, before the final coat. The paint hides the caulk line.

Can caulk soundproof a room?

It helps by sealing the air gaps that sound slips through. It won't replace mass, insulation, or solid-core doors, but it's the cheapest improvement you can make.
